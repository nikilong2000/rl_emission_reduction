\frontmatterchapterheading{Abstract}

This thesis investigates reinforcement-learning-based control strategies for reducing tailpipe \ac{NOx} emissions in a light-duty hybrid diesel vehicle. The work addresses the multi-objective nature of hybrid-electric-vehicle energy management, where speed tracking, emission reduction, battery charge sustainment, and real-time feasibility must be balanced. During training, the controller interacts with a long short-term memory surrogate environment of the vehicle and emission behaviour instead of computationally expensive physical simulations.

The methodology follows a two-stage training and evaluation pipeline. First, \ac{PPO}, \ac{TD3}, and \ac{SAC} are trained for speed tracking to establish stable baseline policies. Their hyperparameters are selected with Optuna and validated across ten random seeds per algorithm. Second, the selected checkpoints are fine-tuned with additional reward terms for \ac{NOx} reduction and charge sustainment. The resulting policies are evaluated on a synthetic staircase cycle, analysed through their engine operating points, and tested zero-shot on the WLTC against a rule-based benchmark controller.

The results show that all three algorithms learn speed tracking, but differ once emission and battery objectives are introduced. \ac{PPO} provides the most robust multi-objective fine-tuning, \ac{SAC} achieves the strongest emission reduction, and \ac{TD3} is limited by training instability. In the WLTC comparison, the best \ac{SAC} seed reaches 80\,mg/km tailpipe \ac{NOx}, compared with 147\,mg/km for the rule-based controller, while also improving speed tracking and maintaining charge sustainment. This improvement comes at the cost of higher fuel consumption, highlighting the remaining trade-off between pollutant reduction and energy efficiency.

Overall, the thesis demonstrates that staged reinforcement learning can produce emission-aware hybrid-vehicle control policies in a surrogate environment and transfer meaningful behaviour to an unseen driving cycle. The results also underline the importance of seed-wise policy selection, explicit fuel objectives, and future validation in a real vehicle or high-fidelity simulation environment.

The full implementation is publicly available on \href{https://github.com/nikilong2000/rl_emission_reduction}{GitHub}.
